Each item below is one post (one storyId, one text). Answer for each.

WHAT A COINED POST LOOKS LIKE
Of 77 posts people actually made coins about, labelled by hand:
  - the picture carries it (82%): the image alone could be the coin's picture
  - there is a character (74%): an animal, a creature, a person turned into a character,
    a mascot, an object with a face or a story
  - it has a one-word name (69%), or a name that reads as one (MOODENG, CHILLGUY)
  - most were NOT jokes (only 36% were funny): serious news and striking real things
    were coined as often as jokes
So the question is not "is this funny" and not "is this a meme yet". It is: is there one
nameable thing here that people could rally a coin around?

YES — one nameable thing, in the words or the picture:
  - an animal or creature with a look, a name or a story (a pygmy hippo, a cat with a job)
  - a character: drawn, from a game or a show, or a person turned into one
  - an object or an absurd real thing (the most expensive cow ever sold, a fish given a
    roller coaster, a cat with its own casino)
  - a phrase or a line people repeat, with or without a picture
  - a product everyone is posting about by name (a new AI model, a gadget)
  - a company's moment (a company joining an index, a launch people repeat)
  - one person's single viral moment (a prize-winner's arrest, a chart a leader held up,
    a line someone said that everyone quotes, a bet someone made in public)
  - a crypto or AI story with a name: a rug pull, an airdrop, a token or model launch, a
    market moment, a product coming to crypto, a crypto figure's line or picture the
    timeline repeats, a joke or pun about a coin or its holders
  - a rallying cry around one person or thing ("JUSTICE FOR ...", "FREE ...", "HELP ..."),
    including a fundraiser or a call to help one named person
  - a picture that is itself the thing. WHEN THE WORDS SAY ALMOST NOTHING — a link, one or
    two words, "good morning", "Always trust" — THE PICTURE DECIDES: if it shows one clear
    figure, creature, character, face or object, answer yes and name what it shows. Only a
    picture of nothing in particular (a crowd, a screenshot of text, scenery) is a no
News IS a subject when one of the things above is at its centre. Name the thing, not the
headline. The test between a person's viral moment and general political news: is the post
about ONE named person or thing and what they did or what happened to them (a viral moment
— yes), or about a policy, an ideology, a party or an election (general — no)?

NO — nothing to coin:
  - sport: a score, a result, a highlight or clip, a transfer, a player's stats or news
    about a player or a team
  - fan accounts: an idol's photo or story, a fancam, a comeback, an album, tour or airport
    update, a streaming or voting goal
  - general political news with nothing in it to name: a policy, an ideology, a party, an
    election, a debate about society
  - a post with nothing specific in it: a mood, a generic photo, a list, an ad
Sport, fandom and politics turn into a YES only when there is a meme subject inside them:
an animal on the pitch, a fan's sign, an object thrown on stage, one line or picture that
people repeat.
Judge what the post shows, never who posted it.

THE NAME
Give a ticker-style name of at most three words, as the post or its picture would have
people say it: the cow is CARINA, the fish on a roller coaster is FISHCOASTER, the hippo
is MOODENG. Not a description of the post. If you cannot name it in three words, answer no.

When you answer yes, give 3 to 5 names in all: the main name, then 2 to 4 alternate names.
  - The first name is ONE word: the subject's own name or nickname, as the post calls it
    (MOODENG, PNUT, CARINA). Only if it has none, the one word people would call it.
  - The others may be short phrases or words run together (FISHCOASTER), each a name a
    coin could carry, never a sentence or a description.
  - Never start a name with "the", "a", "an" or "my".
  - Never a brand, a company, a product line or a theme park as a name, even when it is
    in the picture or the post is about a product: name the creature, the character, the
    moment or the nickname people give it, not the logo behind it.
  - Never a real person's full name. A real person may be named only by the nickname the
    post itself gives them.
When you answer no, give no names.

HOW SURE ARE YOU?
A number between 0 and 1, given only this post. High when one thing clearly stands out;
low when the post could be about several things.

Do not consider how many people saw the post. Numbers of views, likes or shares are not
shown to you, deliberately: attention is measured elsewhere.

THE OWNER'S RULE FOR NEWS, WHICH OVERRIDES THE NEWS LINES ABOVE
For You is one feed for anything people could coin: memes, news and tragedies alike. If there is ONE nameable thing at the centre of the post — a named person, ride, place, company milestone, product, incident or phrase — answer yes and name it, even when the post is news, politics, business or a death. Use what you already know about who or what that named thing is. Answer no only when the post is generic: news with no single nameable thing in it, a list, a schedule, a mood, an ad, or a post about several things at once.
A real person may still be named only by a nickname or one word people would use (the ride GOLIATH, not the victim's name).

FAN POSTS ABOUT CHARACTERS THAT ALREADY EXIST, WHICH OVERRIDES THE CHARACTER LINE ABOVE
A character who already belongs to a show, film, comic, cartoon, anime or game — a superhero,
an anime or cartoon character, a game boss, a franchise's mascot — is not a new subject when
fans post it: fan art, a comic panel or screenshot, a reaction image, a ship, a cosplay, a joke
caption over a known character. Answer no, unless something new happened to it that people
outside the fans are repeating. The character line above is for characters that are new or that
the post itself makes: an original drawing, an animal, a mascot nobody owned before.
One thing is new by itself: a short, playful claim that a well-known character, mascot or show
belongs to a country or a people ("do the Japanese know Vegeta is Algerian?", "Mario is Mexican"),
or a taunt that sets one country's fans against another's over one. Such a claim starts a meme war
between the two sides, and people coin it. Answer yes and name the character first, then the claim
run together (VEGETA, ALGERIANVEGETA). A slur, an insult to a people, a call to keep anyone out,
or news or politics about nationality, migration or race is not this and is judged by the lines
above.

ALSO YES, IN ANY LANGUAGE
  - a famous person's death, told as news: the person is the subject. Name them by the one
    word people would use — a first name, a nickname or the role they are known for — never
    their full name
  - an animal rescue, a zoo, a shelter or a sanctuary in trouble or asking for help: the
    animals or the place are the subject
  - a creator's own recurring character or persona, named in the post ("he did it again",
    "X struck again"): that character is the subject

CRYPTO'S OWN RECURRING OMENS, WHICH OVERRIDE THE BRAND AND PRODUCT LINES ABOVE
A few ordinary events have been read by crypto people for years as a sign for the market, and a
coin is made the moment one is announced, even when the post itself says nothing about crypto.
When a post announces or reports one of these happening, answer yes and give the omen's name first:
  - the McRib, by that name, coming back to McDonald's menus anywhere: a return, a date, a first
    sighting ("the McRib indicator": bitcoin is said to rise when it returns) — MCRIB
Only the omens listed here get this exception, and only for the event itself. Never give an
omen's name to a post that does not name it. A post that only jokes about an omen, compares
something else to it ("the next McRib", "a better index than the McRib") or pushes another coin
to pair with it is judged on its own subject, by the lines above. Any other menu item, product or
brand coming back, a price change, a sale or a new flavour is still brand news and judged by the
lines above.

THE OWNER'S RULES, WHICH OVERRIDE EVERY LINE ABOVE (the news rule included)
  - A tragedy is a meme only if an animal, a famous person, a world leader or something absurd is
    at its centre. Ordinary people killed, hurt or arrested in crime, accidents, fires, attacks or
    disasters: answer no.
  - Politics is a meme only when one world-famous figure says or does one striking, repeatable
    thing: a line, an object, a nickname. Election day, voting, chants, hand signs, crowds or
    partisan jokes: answer no.
  - Promotions are not memes: a game, film, show, toy or brand announcement, a #pr post, an artist
    promoting their own work, a crypto post pushing a coin, a meta or its poster's own trades.
  - Sport is not a meme: a match moment, an injury, a substitution, a team or league account's
    post, fans reacting to a result.
  - An ordinary pet, baby, zoo animal, kid or kind stranger doing something sweet is not a meme
    without a name, a catchphrase or something strange about it.
  - Fans of existing characters are not memes: a VTuber, an original character or sona, an anime,
    game or monster-collecting character, a celebrity posting about their own role.
